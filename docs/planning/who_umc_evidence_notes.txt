EVIDENCE 1: PURPOSE AND LIMITATIONS OF CAUSALITY ASSESSMENT
PDF page: 1
Evidence:
The WHO-UMC system provides a structured and harmonised approach to assessing suspected adverse drug reaction case reports. It helps classify relationship likelihood and reduce disagreement between assessors. It cannot prove a connection between a drug and an event, provide an accurate quantitative measurement of likelihood, or change uncertainty into certainty.

EVIDENCE 2: CERTAIN
PDF page: 2
Evidence:
The event must have a plausible time relationship to drug intake, cannot be explained by disease or other drugs, and should have a plausible response to withdrawal. The event must also be definitive pharmacologically or phenomenologically. Rechallenge should be satisfactory if necessary.

EVIDENCE 3: PROBABLE / LIKELY
PDF page: 2
Evidence:
The event must have a reasonable time relationship to drug intake, be unlikely to be attributed to disease or other drugs, and have a clinically reasonable response to withdrawal. Rechallenge is not required.

EVIDENCE 4: POSSIBLE
PDF page: 2
Evidence:
The event must have a reasonable time relationship to drug intake, but disease or other drugs could also explain it. Information about what happened after drug withdrawal may be missing or unclear.

EVIDENCE 5: UNLIKELY
PDF page: 2
Evidence:
The time relationship makes a connection improbable, although not impossible, and disease or other drugs provide plausible explanations.

EVIDENCE 6: CONDITIONAL / UNCLASSIFIED
PDF page: 2
Evidence:
More information is needed for proper assessment, or additional information is still being examined.

EVIDENCE 7: UNASSESSABLE / UNCLASSIFIABLE
PDF page: 2
Evidence:
The report cannot be assessed because information is insufficient or contradictory, and the missing information cannot be supplemented or verified.

EVIDENCE 8: DISTINGUISHING PROBABLE FROM POSSIBLE
PDF page: 3
Evidence:
A possible case may have another equally likely explanation and/or may lack clear information about what happened after drug withdrawal. A probable case requires alternative causes to be unlikely and the withdrawal response to be clinically reasonable.

IMPLEMENTATION NOTE:
The WHO-UMC categories are evidence-guided assessment categories, not proof of causation. PharmaSafe AI must not independently establish medical causality. It may organise the available information, show the relevant criteria and supporting source passages, and flag missing or contradictory information for qualified human review.
